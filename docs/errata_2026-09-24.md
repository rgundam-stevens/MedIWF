# Errata and clarifications to the dated study documents (24 September 2026)

The protocol, its amendments, the detector changelog, the hand-check log and the rater guide are released as they were
written. This file records what later checks found in them; the documents themselves are unchanged.

1. Amendment 5 (20 September), A23 and A25, and Amendment 6 (22 September), status line, A27 and the GPT-5.4 Mini/Gemini
   extension: "reasoning disabled". The judge calls asked the gateway to disable hidden reasoning (src/mediwf/openrouter.py,
   reasoning: enabled false), which not every model accepts. GPT-5.4 Mini recorded 0 reasoning tokens on the 100
   physician-rated items. Gemini 3.1 Pro, whose interface does not allow reasoning to be disabled, recorded reasoning tokens
   on all 100 physician-rated items (per-item totals over the 4 rule calls; median 1,325). Reasoning tokens were not recorded
   on the 216 course items for any judge, nor for Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra on the physician-rated items, so whether
   those two judges reasoned is unknown. The paper describes the setting as a request to disable hidden reasoning, with the
   outcome per judge. Amendment 1 had already noted that five of the seven flagship models reject the request to disable
   reasoning.

2. Protocol (13 September), section "Ethics, employer, and disclosure" ("his employment agreement ... a short written
   acknowledgement from ABEM") and item 2 of the closing list ("ABEM employment agreement").
   The author is not employed by the American Board of Emergency Medicine; he is employed by a contracting
   firm and works at the Board as a software contractor. The agreement referred to is his agreement with that employer.
   The paper's conflict-of-interest statement describes the relationship in those terms.

3. Amendment 2 (16 September), A6: "kappa <= 0.43 on every rule" describes the per-rule judges GPT-5.4 Mini and Gemini
   3.1 Pro (outputs/judge_validation_gpt54mini_v2.txt, judge_validation_gemini31pro_v1.txt). An earlier run of a single
   combined 9-rule prompt (scripts/judge.py; data/generated/judge_*.jsonl, 14 September) reaches kappa 0.57 for "no
   extraneous information" with GPT-5.4 Mini against the de-duplicated labels of validate_judge.py v2; Supplement 1 of the
   paper reports it.

4. Amendment 6 (22 September), note 4 and deviation 16: the sentence disclosing generative-AI assistance was planned for the
   manuscript's Acknowledgments. Journals differ in where they allow such a statement; where a journal does not allow generative
   AI to be named in the Acknowledgments, the disclosure is made in the letter to the editor instead and the manuscript carries
   no such sentence.
