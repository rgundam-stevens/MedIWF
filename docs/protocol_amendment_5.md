# Protocol Amendment 5 (20 September 2026)

Written before any of the work below was started. Status of the study at the time: hand-verification pass 1 complete (20 Sep); physician content ratings: rater 1 complete, rater 2 pending; stability re-run scheduled for 28 Sep; manuscript draft v0.5.

## A22. Inter-rater reliability replaces the author's intra-rater pass 2
The protocol planned a second reading of 150 hand-verification items by the author at least four weeks after pass 1 (intra-rater agreement). This is replaced by an inter-rater design: one or two additional raters, independent of the study and not affiliated with the author's employer, label the full 439-item sample with the same one-sheet-per-rule workbook (identical helper cells, empty answer columns), the same Rater Guide v3 and the same practice set, blind to the detector's output and to the author's labels, without discussion of items with the author while labelling and without AI tools. Each rater first completes a 10-item screening test of invented items with planted flaws (docs/structural_screen_10_for_chat.txt; key in docs/structural_screen_10_KEY_DO_NOT_SEND.md; pass = at least 7 of 10 items exactly right and both clean items unflagged). Raters may be people known to the author; they are described in the paper as independent non-clinician raters and their labels are reported anonymously with their consent. Analysis (scripts/analyze_verification.py, to be extended): per-rule agreement between the author and each rater and among raters (Cohen's kappa; Fleiss' kappa when three sets exist), and the detector's design-based precision and recall against each rater's labels and against the majority label. Reason for the change: independent raters give a stronger reliability estimate than a self re-read, answer the 'self-check' limitation directly, and avoid a four-week delay. The author's pass 2 is dropped; the 19 October reminder is cancelled.

## A23. Execution of A14 (stronger LLM judges) with a cost-capped procedure
A14 of Amendment 3 is run as written: BenchMarker per-rule prompts, the same 216 human-labelled examination items and nine rules, temperature 0, seed 7, reasoning disabled, one call per item and rule, judges Claude Fable 5.1 (anthropic/claude-fable-5.1) and GPT-6 Astra (openai/gpt-6-astra), both accessed through OpenRouter. Procedure: a pilot of the first 12 items per judge with a cost cap of US $5 (scripts/judge_perrule.py --limit 12 --max-cost 5), from which the full-run cost is projected; the full run only if the projection is at or below US $45 per judge, executed with --max-cost 45 (resumable). Scoring by scripts/validate_judge.py, unchanged. Hypothesis unchanged from A14: agreement with the human labels remains weak (kappa < 0.5 on every rule). Pre-stated reading of the alternative: kappa >= 0.6 on three or more rules for a judge is reported as 'frontier judges approach human agreement on those rules' and the paper's judge conclusion is revised accordingly. Disclosures: both judges are among the fifteen corpus generators, and one is supplied by the vendor of the AI assistant used in this study; the judged items are BenchMarker's, not ours. Budget: at most US $100 in total (pilot plus full runs); the cost cap is enforced by the script.

Pilot outcome and stage plan (recorded 20 September, before the full runs). Pilot of 12 items x 9 rules per judge: Fable 5.1 US $3.125 (US $0.260 per item; projected US $56.26 for all 216 items); GPT-6 Astra US $1.571 (US $0.131 per item; projected US $28.28). Fable's projection exceeds the US $45 per-judge threshold above. Rather than drop that judge, the per-judge threshold is replaced by one overall cap equal to the funds set aside for this task, US $88 (within the US $100 budget), and the work is split into three cost-capped stages run in this order: (1) Fable 5.1 on the five content rules (single_best_answer, plausible_distractors, no_extraneous_info, focused_stem, problem_in_stem), cap US $31, projected US $29.50 for the 204 remaining items; (2) GPT-6 Astra on all nine rules, cap US $28, projected US $26.70; (3) Fable 5.1 on the four lexical rules (clear_language, no_absolute_terms, no_vague_terms, no_logical_cues), projected US $23.60, run only if the funds left after stages 1 and 2 cover the projection; otherwise Fable 5.1 is reported on the content rules only and this is stated. Content rules go first because they are the rules on which a judge could plausibly outperform the lexical detector; the lexical rules are near-deterministic word checks. The 12 pilot items are not re-run (the script skips completed items). Pilot spend: US $4.70.

## A25. Reasoning-on sensitivity check (conditional; decision rule fixed before the A23 results are seen)
The A23 judges run with reasoning disabled, as did the September judges (GPT-5.4 Mini, Gemini 3.1 Pro) and BenchMarker's own setup, so that the results are comparable and the cost is bounded. This leaves one obvious objection to a negative result: that the judges were handicapped. The following rule is fixed now so that the decision does not depend on the results. (i) If, with reasoning off, neither frontier judge reaches kappa >= 0.5 on any of the five content rules, a reasoning-on check is run for the judge with the higher mean content-rule kappa: the same prompts, temperature 0, seed 7, reasoning enabled, five content rules only, on a fixed random subsample of 60 of the 216 items (drawn with seed 7 and listed in the output), preceded by a 5-item pilot with a cost cap of US $5 from which the cost is projected; the full check runs only if the projection fits a separate budget approved by the author for this purpose (not part of the US $88), enforced with --max-cost. It is reported as a sensitivity check (kappa with reasoning on versus off on the same 60 items, with confidence intervals); with 60 items it can show a large change, not a small one, and it does not replace the full-sample estimates. (ii) If either judge reaches kappa >= 0.5 on at least one content rule with reasoning off, no reasoning-on run is made: the reasoning-off result already changes the paper's judge conclusion, and whether reasoning improves it further is left as future work. The four lexical rules are excluded from the check because reasoning is not expected to matter for word-list rules. Before any such run, scripts/judge_perrule.py gains two switches (--reasoning, passing reasoning enabled to the client, and --subsample N with a seed) and each is tested on one item first.
