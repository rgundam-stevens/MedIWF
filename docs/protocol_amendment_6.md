# Protocol Amendment 6 (22 September 2026)

Written before any of the work below was started, after three simulated referee reviews of manuscript v0.6 (docs/reviews_v0.6/) and the no-cost revisions of manuscript v0.7. Status at the time: hand-verification pass 1 complete (author); physician content ratings complete (two raters, 100 items); four LLM judges complete on the 216 BenchMarker examination items (reasoning disabled); stability re-run scheduled for 28 September.

## A26. Independent structural raters label from the item text alone (revises A22)
The raters of A22 will receive the one-sheet-per-rule workbook WITHOUT the computed helper cells (no option lengths, no length arithmetic, no word lists, no containment pairs): each sheet shows the sample id, the fields the rule needs (options; stem and options where the rule concerns the stem), an empty 0/1 answer column and a notes column, with the rule stated in construct terms at the top of the sheet (for example, "the correct option is noticeably longer than every wrong option", not the 25% arithmetic). Everything else in A22 stands (screening test, blinding to the detector and to the author's labels, no AI tools, anonymous reporting with consent). Reason: the referees observed that for the arithmetic rules the author's helper-cell check confirms the implementation, not the construct; raters working from the text alone test the construct. Analysis adds: agreement between each rater and the author, and the detector's precision and recall against each rater and the majority label, with the author's helper-cell check reported separately as an implementation check.

Note added 22 September 2026: the author states that during his own pass (Amendment 4 A21 workbook) the helper columns were collapsed while labelling and opened only after each sheet was complete (docs/hand_check_pass1_log.md, rater's statement). The requirement of A26 for the independent raters is unchanged; the description of the author's pass in the manuscript is corrected (v0.8.5).

## A27. LLM judges on the 100 physician-rated corpus items (reasoning disabled)
Design: the two frontier judges of A23 (Claude Fable 5.1, GPT-6 Astra) judge the 100 corpus items rated by the two physicians (outputs/content_rating/main_set_100.xlsx), with the same BenchMarker per-rule prompts, temperature 0, seed 7, hidden reasoning disabled, one call per item and rule, for the four content rules that have a physician counterpart or contribute to the composite (single_best_answer, plausible_distractors, no_extraneous_info, focused_stem). Script: scripts/judge_perrule.py --physician; scoring: scripts/validate_judge_physician.py. Cost caps enforced by the script: US $14 (Fable) and US $9 (Astra); expected about $10 and $6.
Reference labels (fixed now): primary, each physician separately (rater 1: 13 composite defects; rater 2: 12); secondary, items flagged by both raters (8) and by either (17). Mappings: the judge's single_best_answer verdict against Q1 not "yes"; plausible_distractors against Q2 "no"; any of the four rules against the composite defect (Q1 not yes, Q2 no, Q3 no or Q5 no) and, secondarily, against Q5 "no". Statistics: sensitivity, specificity, precision and Cohen's kappa with bootstrap intervals, per judge and reference; judge-versus-judge agreement per rule.
Hypotheses (consistent with A14/A23): primary, for each judge and each physician, kappa against the composite < 0.5 and sensitivity for the composite defect <= 0.5 (the judges miss at least half of the defects a physician found). Pre-stated alternative reading: kappa >= 0.6 against both physicians, or sensitivity >= 0.8 with specificity >= 0.8, is reported as "the judges screen medical content comparably to junior physicians" and the paper's judge conclusion is revised. With 12-13 positives the intervals will be wide; the result is reported as a screen with intervals, not as a precise estimate. Items are LLM-generated corpus items (no NBME material). Disclosure unchanged: both judges are corpus generators; one is supplied by the vendor of the AI assistant used in the study; the judges may have generated some of the 100 items (the item's generator is recorded and the analysis will report agreement with and without each judge's own items).

## A28. Reasoning-enabled judge check on the full 216-item set (revises A25; subject to funding)
If funded, the A25 check runs on all 216 examination items rather than a 60-item subsample: judge Claude Fable 5.1 (the higher mean content-rule kappa), the four scorable content rules, temperature as the API requires when reasoning is enabled (recorded per call), seed 7 where honoured, one call per item and rule, reasoning enabled. Before the run: (i) the client's fallback on a rejected request is changed so that the reasoning setting is never dropped silently (a request that cannot be served with reasoning fails and is logged); (ii) a 12-item pilot with a cost cap of US $10, from which the full cost is projected; (iii) the full run only if the projection is within the ceiling the author records here before the pilot: ceiling US $138 in total (the author's OpenRouter balance of US $138.73 on 22 September, committed to this check; pilot cap US $15, full-run cap US $120, the remainder reserved for gap-filling and the 28 September stability re-run); thinking budget: none imposed at the pilot (effort 'high', answer budget 16,000 tokens); after the pilot the author may set a per-call thinking budget if the projection exceeds the ceiling, and that choice is recorded here before the full run; (iv) every record must show reasoning tokens > 0, checked after the pilot and after the full run. Analysis: the same kappa table as Table 5 with bootstrap intervals, paired with the reasoning-off verdicts item by item (McNemar test per rule on agreement with the human label), and the change in mean content-rule kappa. Hypothesis: kappa with reasoning enabled remains < 0.5 on every content rule; alternative: kappa >= 0.6 on at least two content rules is reported as "reasoning changes the judge conclusion". If A28 is funded, the same reasoning-enabled run on the 100 physician-rated items (A27 design) is added at the author's discretion, recorded here before it runs.

## A27 outcome (recorded 22 September 2026, after the run)
Runs: Claude Fable 5.1 US $11.26 (99 of 100 items; for MAIN-047 the provider's content filter returned empty responses for three of the four rules on every attempt, at no cost; the item is excluded from Fable's comparisons and reported as a refusal); GPT-6 Astra US $6.22 (100 of 100). Results: outputs/judge_physician100_validation_v1.txt.
Against the pre-stated hypotheses: the primary hypothesis (kappa < 0.5 and sensitivity <= 0.5 for each judge and each physician) is NOT confirmed for Fable 5.1 (composite: kappa 0.53 [0.25, 0.75] against rater 1 and 0.56 [0.28, 0.78] against rater 2, sensitivity 0.62 and 0.67, specificity 0.93; single best answer against rater 1: kappa 0.71 [0.42, 0.92], 7 of 11 physician flags found with 1 false alarm) and is confirmed for GPT-6 Astra on the composite only (kappa 0.15 and 0.28), because Astra flagged "extraneous information" in 21% of items, a rule the physicians were not asked about; on the single-best-answer rule Astra reached kappa 0.56 [0.26, 0.79] against rater 1. The pre-stated alternative reading (kappa >= 0.6 against both physicians, or sensitivity >= 0.8 with specificity >= 0.8) is not met either. The result therefore lies between the two readings: on these medical items, one frontier judge with a single prompt agrees with each junior physician about as well as the two physicians agree with each other (kappa 0.59), whereas on the educator-written examination items it had been at chance on the same rule. Excluding each judge's own generated items changed nothing (Fable: 0.75 and 0.55; Astra: 0.55 and 0.14). Judge-versus-judge agreement on single best answer: kappa 0.67. Interpretation for the paper: the judge conclusion is revised from "LLM judges do not reproduce human content judgment" to "LLM judges did not reproduce the published labels on educator-written items but one of them approached junior-physician agreement on the single-best-answer question in medical items, with wide intervals (11-13 physician flags)"; the reasoning-enabled check (A28) becomes more, not less, informative.

## A28 pilot outcome and full-run decision (recorded 22 September 2026, before the full run)
Two 12-item pilots (48 calls each): reasoning enabled at effort "high" with no thinking budget, US $1.40; the same with a 4,000-token thinking budget, US $1.38. In both, every response reported a reasoning field and temperature 0 was accepted, and the model used thinking on 21 of 48 calls (44%), with a median of about 300 thinking tokens when it did; the budget did not change how often or how long it thought (adaptive thinking: the model decides per call). Projected cost for 216 items: about US $25. Decision: the full run uses effort "high" with no thinking budget (the amendment's stated default), cap US $40; the budget pilot is kept as a same-day replicate on the same items. Interpretation to be reported: "reasoning enabled" for this model means the model may think, and it chose to on fewer than half the calls, so the check tests the model's own judgement of how much thinking the task needs, not forced deliberation; the API offers no way to force thinking beyond offering a budget. Because the projection is far below the ceiling, the same reasoning-enabled run on the 100 physician-rated items (A27 design, projected about US $12) is added, cap US $25.

## A28 outcome (recorded 22 September 2026, after the runs)
Runs: 216 BenchMarker items, four content rules, reasoning enabled, US $21.85 (all 216 complete); 100 physician-rated items, US $11.25 (99 complete; MAIN-047 again refused by the provider's content filter). Fable 5.1 used thinking on 29% of the 864 calls (median 277 tokens, maximum 890) and on 22% of the 396 calls on the physician-rated items (median 269, maximum 1,280). Results: outputs/judge_validation_fable51_reasoning_v1.txt, outputs/judge_reasoning_comparison_benchmarker_v1.txt, outputs/judge_reasoning_comparison_physician_v1.txt.
Against the pre-stated hypothesis: confirmed. With reasoning enabled the verdicts were identical to the reasoning-disabled verdicts on 96-98% of items per rule; kappa against the human labels was 0.02, 0.38, 0.29 and 0.31 (single best answer, plausible distractors, extraneous information, focused stem) against 0.05, 0.48, 0.29 and 0.33 with reasoning disabled, no rule reached 0.5, and no McNemar test approached significance (p >= 0.375). On the physician-rated items the picture is the same: composite kappa 0.56 and 0.59 against the two raters with reasoning (0.53 and 0.56 without), single-best-answer kappa 0.67 against rater 1 (0.71 without). The alternative reading (kappa >= 0.6 on two content rules) is not met. Interpretation: enabling reasoning did not change the judge's agreement with either the published labels or the physicians; the earlier results were not an artefact of the setting. Caveat to report: the model engaged thinking on fewer than a third of calls and briefly, so this tests the model's own allocation of thought, not forced deliberation. Cost note: the reasoning-enabled run of the four content rules (US $21.85) was cheaper than the reasoning-disabled nine-rule run because it covered fewer rules; per call the two settings cost about the same. Total A28 spend as recorded by scripts/costs.py: US $34.48 (the two 12-item pilots and the 4,000-budget replicate included; the first pilot's file, set aside after the verdict-keeping rule was corrected, cost US $1.33 and is kept in outputs/_to_delete/). Amendment 6 spend in total: US $51.96 recorded, about US $53 with the set-aside pilot.

## A29. GPT-6 Astra with reasoning enabled (added 22 September 2026, before running)
Reason: Fable 5.1 allocates thinking adaptively and used it on fewer than a third of calls, so A28 did not test forced deliberation; OpenAI reasoning models reason on every call at effort "high", so an Astra run is the direct test of the referees' objection. Design as A28 (216 BenchMarker items, four content rules, reasoning effort high, temperature 0 where accepted, seed 7, verdict kept only if the response reports reasoning; separate output file). Hypothesis unchanged (kappa < 0.5 on every content rule; alternative kappa >= 0.6 on at least two). Procedure: 12-item pilot capped at US $15; the full 216-item run only if the projection is at or below the balance minus a US $10 reserve for the stability re-run; if it is not, a random 100-item subsample of the 216 (seed 7, listed in the output) is run instead, cap set from the projection, and reported as such. The physician-rated items are not run for Astra under reasoning unless the balance allows it after the 216-item run (recorded here before running).

## A27 extension (added 22 September 2026, before running): the two earlier judges on the physician-rated items
GPT-5.4 Mini and Gemini 3.1 Pro are run on the same 100 items with the same four content rules and settings (reasoning disabled), so that the physician comparison has the same four judges as the examination-item comparison; scored by the same script and against the same references and hypotheses as A27. Expected cost about US $4 in total (caps US $3 and US $6). Purpose: to test whether the near-physician agreement seen for Fable 5.1 is specific to that model or shared by judges from other vendors and price tiers.

## A27 extension outcome (recorded 22 September 2026)
GPT-5.4 Mini US $0.43 and Gemini 3.1 Pro US $2.95, 100 of 100 items each. Both confirm the primary hypothesis: GPT-5.4 Mini never flagged a single-best-answer defect (0 of 100; sensitivity 0 against both physicians; composite kappa 0.28 and 0.05), and Gemini 3.1 Pro found 3 of rater 1's 11 single-best-answer flags (kappa 0.33 [-0.03, 0.63]; composite kappa 0.22 and 0.24). The near-physician agreement of Fable 5.1 (single best answer kappa 0.71 against rater 1; composite 0.53 and 0.56) is therefore specific to that judge among the four; GPT-6 Astra is intermediate (single best answer 0.56 against rater 1; composite 0.15 and 0.28, pulled down by its 21% extraneous-information flags). Results in outputs/judge_physician100_validation_v1.txt.

## A29 pilot outcome and decision (recorded 22 September 2026, before the full run)
Pilot: 12 items, US $0.83, every response reported reasoning, temperature 0 accepted; Astra used thinking on 38 of 48 calls (79%), median 87 thinking tokens. Projection for 216 items: US $15, within the rule (balance minus the US $10 reserve), so the full run proceeds with cap US $20. Astra's thinking is brief (a median of under 100 tokens), so this run, like A28, tests the model's own allocation of thought at effort "high"; that is recorded as a limitation of both reasoning checks. Given the low projection, the reasoning-enabled run on the 100 physician-rated items is added for Astra as well (projected about US $7, cap US $12), so that both frontier judges have the same four comparisons.

## A29 outcome (recorded 22 September 2026, after the runs)
GPT-6 Astra with reasoning enabled: 216 BenchMarker items US $13.98 (all complete), 100 physician-rated items US $7.67 (all complete); thinking used on 83% and 81% of calls, median about 60 tokens, maximum about 900. Hypothesis confirmed: kappa against the published labels -0.02, 0.42, 0.31 and 0.30 (single best answer, plausible distractors, extraneous information, focused stem) against -0.01, 0.40, 0.26 and 0.33 with reasoning disabled; no rule reached 0.5; no McNemar test significant (smallest p = 0.125). On the physician-rated items the single-best-answer agreement was identical in both settings (kappa 0.56 against rater 1, 0.37 against rater 2) and the composite fell slightly (0.11 and 0.23 against 0.15 and 0.28) because the extraneous-information flags rose further. Results: outputs/judge_validation_gpt6astra_reasoning_v1.txt, outputs/judge_reasoning_comparison_benchmarker_astra_v1.txt, outputs/judge_reasoning_comparison_physician_astra_v1.txt. Conclusion of the reasoning checks (A28, A29): for both frontier judges, enabling reasoning left agreement with humans unchanged on both item sets; both models allocated little thinking to these calls, which is recorded as a limitation of the checks.
