# Protocol amendment 7: independent reader check of the three most frequently flagged rules

Design recorded 21 October 2026 (11:10 EDT), before the readers received the sheets; conduct and result recorded the same evening.

Purpose. The author's hand-check of 23 September (hand_check_pass1_log.md) labelled the longest-option and word-repetition rules with the rules' helper columns in view and was done by the author alone, so it shows that the detector applies its own definitions rather than that independent readers regard the flagged features as flaws. This amendment adds a reader check of the three rules that account for most flagged items.

Sample. 120 items from the 12,000 analysed main-corpus items, drawn by script (scripts/build_independent_check.py, seed 20261021) from five strata: 30 items flagged only by the longest-option rule, 30 only by the word-repetition rule, 20 only by the absolute-terms rule, 10 flagged by two or three of these rules, and 30 flagged by none of the three (other rules may fire); within each stratum half plain-prompt and half guided-prompt items; all 15 models represented; random order; identifiers IND-001 to IND-120. Readers see the stem, the five options and the keyed answer only: no model, prompt, rule or flag information. The detector is positive on 40 items for longest option, 38 for word repetition and 23 for absolute terms.

Readers and questions. Two master's graduates not involved in the study, unpaid, who had not seen the manuscript, the detector or its output (reader 1, reader 2), each working alone on their own computer. A one-page instruction sheet (MedIWF_independent_check_instructions.md) gives plain-language definitions and one example each way for the three questions but not the detector's thresholds. Q1 longest option: is the correct answer noticeably longer or more detailed than the other options; Q2 word repetition: does the stem contain a word or phrase repeated in the correct answer and in none of the other options, so that matching words would point to the answer; Q3 absolute terms: does any option use absolute wording such as always, never, all, none, only, every, completely. Answers yes, no or unsure.

Analysis, stated in advance (scripts/analyze_independent_check.py). For each rule: Cohen's kappa between the readers; the detector's raw agreement, kappa, precision and recall against each reader and against the reader consensus (both yes), with 95% bootstrap intervals (2,000 resamples, seed 20261021); "unsure" counted as "no" in the main analysis, with "unsure" as "yes" and "unsure" dropped as sensitivity analyses. Interpretation stated in advance: kappa of 0.6 or more between the detector and the reader consensus is read as substantial agreement, 0.4 to 0.6 as moderate, below 0.4 as poor; the result is reported whatever it is. Because the sample is enriched for flagged items, prevalence is not estimated from it.

Conduct. A first pair of readers rated the sample on 21 October 2026; their sheets were set aside and the check was repeated the same day with a second pair of readers, whose ratings are reported. Reader 1 and reader 2 each returned a completed sheet by e-mail on 21 October 2026 (all 120 items answered; reader 1 marked 1 item "unsure" on Q2), with a written confirmation that they worked alone without an AI tool and preferred not to be named. The sheets as returned are the data of record and are released as reader 1 and reader 2 (outputs/independent_check/MedIWF_independent_check_ratings.csv, with the detector's flags); the first pair's sheets are retained in the project records and not released.

Result (unsure counted as no). Kappa between readers: longest option 0.98 (95% CI 0.93 to 1.00), word repetition 0.96 (0.89 to 1.00), absolute terms 0.97 (0.89 to 1.00). Detector against the reader consensus: longest option kappa 0.94 (0.87 to 1.00), precision 0.95, recall 0.97; word repetition kappa 0.90 (0.81 to 0.98), precision 0.89, recall 0.97; absolute terms kappa 0.92 (0.80 to 1.00), precision 0.87, recall 1.00. The sensitivity analyses (unsure as yes; unsure dropped) move no kappa by more than 0.02. The detector's word-repetition false positives are single shared words that the readers did not see as cues ("diffuse", "hospitalization", "acid"); its misses are stem words repeated in the key in another grammatical form (pelvic/pelvis; lymphocytes/lymphocytic, seen by 1 reader), which its lemmatiser does not join. Its absolute-terms false positives are "completely symptom-free" in a distractor and "all" and "only" used as quantifiers in 2 distractors of 1 item, which neither reader read as absolute claims; by eye both readers counted 1 key as the longest that the rule's 80% threshold does not, and neither counted 1 that it does.

Addendum, 22 October 2026. (1) Reason for setting aside the first pair: their 2 sheets were returned from the same computer, so the independence of the 2 ratings could not be verified. Their ratings as received gave κ between readers of 0.93 (longest option), 0.94 (word repetition) and 0.97 (absolute terms), and detector-vs-consensus κ of 0.94, 0.84 and 0.94 (same analysis script, unsure as no); they are recorded here for completeness and were not used. (2) The second pair's readers met the criteria in the "Readers and questions" paragraph and consented in writing, by e-mail, to the release of their anonymous ratings. (3) Design-weighted check, post hoc: weighting each item by its stratum's corpus count divided by its sample count (unflagged stratum 10,068 of 12,000 items; flagged strata 751, 665, 267 and 249), the detector's precision and recall against the consensus were 0.95 and 0.98 (longest option), 0.90 and 0.97 (word repetition) and 0.85 and 1.00 (absolute terms); no consensus miss fell in the unflagged stratum. (4) Correction to the Purpose paragraph: "with the rules' helper columns in view" should read "with helper columns available in the workbook, kept collapsed while labelling, as hand_check_pass1_log.md records".
