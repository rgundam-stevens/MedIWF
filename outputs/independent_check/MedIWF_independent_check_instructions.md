# Independent reader check of three item-writing features

Thank you for helping. This should take about 60 to 90 minutes.

## What this is

The attached spreadsheet holds 120 medical multiple-choice questions written by AI models for a research study on question quality. Each item has a question (the stem), five options A to E, and the option the item writer marked as correct. You are asked to read each item and answer three yes/no questions about the wording and shape of the options. You do not need medical knowledge, and you should not judge whether the marked answer is medically right; the three questions are only about form.

You were asked because you had no part in the study and have not seen how these items were chosen. The study needs readers who judge independently, so please keep to the rules below.

## Rules

Work alone. Do not discuss any item with anyone, including the other reader and the person who sent you the sheet, until both sheets have been returned. Do not use any AI tool, and do not search the web for the questions; nothing needs to be looked up. Read at normal speed and give your first considered answer; where you cannot decide, choose "unsure" rather than guessing. Answer every item.

## The three questions

**Q1. Longest option.** Test-wise examinees tend to pick the longest, most detailed option, because item writers often make the correct answer the most complete and carefully qualified one. Answer **yes** if the correct answer is noticeably longer or more detailed than the other four options; answer **no** if the options are of similar length or the correct answer is not the longest. "Noticeably" means a reader would spot it without counting words.

Example, yes: correct answer "Start intravenous ceftriaxone and vancomycin after obtaining blood cultures and a lumbar puncture"; other options "Oral amoxicillin", "Observation", "CT of the head", "Acyclovir".
Example, no: correct answer "Metformin"; other options "Insulin glargine", "Sitagliptin", "Empagliflozin", "Glipizide".

**Q2. Word repetition.** Answer **yes** if the stem contains a word or phrase that reappears in the correct answer and in none of the other options, so that matching words would point a reader to the answer. Ignore generic words that any option could contain (patient, treatment, therapy, management, disease). Answer **no** if the repeated word also appears in other options, or if nothing specific is repeated.

Example, yes: the stem describes a driver with a "seat-belt sign" after a collision; the correct answer reads "CT of the abdomen with contrast to evaluate the seat-belt injury" and no other option mentions the seat belt.
Example, no: the stem mentions "chest pain" and three of the five options mention chest pain.

**Q3. Absolute terms.** Answer **yes** if any option (correct or not) uses absolute wording such as always, never, all, none, only, every, completely, entirely, impossible or must, because test-wise examinees treat such options as likely to be false. Answer **no** if no option uses such a word, or if the word is part of a fixed name or standard phrase (for example "all-trans retinoic acid").

Example, yes: an option reads "Surgery is never indicated in this condition".
Example, no: options describe doses, tests and drugs without any absolute word.

## How to fill the sheet

Columns I, J and K have a drop-down list: yes, no, unsure. Column L is for an optional comment. Please save the file with your initials added to the file name and return it. If you like, note how long the task took.

## Consent

Your answers will be analysed and released with the study's data as anonymous ratings ("reader A" and "reader B"). If you would rather be named in the acknowledgements, say so; otherwise you stay anonymous. Please reply "I agree" to confirm that this is acceptable and that you worked alone without an AI tool.
